# Crisis Response Team Hiring Guide

**Purpose:** Build a qualified, licensed team to handle mental health crisis interventions

**⚠️ CRITICAL:** Lives depend on having qualified professionals available 24/7

---

## 🎯 Team Structure & Roles

### **1. Crisis Clinicians (Licensed Mental Health Professionals)**

**Required Qualifications:**
- ✅ Current, unrestricted clinical license (LCSW, LMFT, LPCC, PhD, PsyD)
- ✅ Minimum 3-5 years post-licensure experience
- ✅ Experience in crisis intervention, suicide assessment, emergency psychiatry
- ✅ Training in evidence-based suicide prevention models
- ✅ Clean malpractice history
- ✅ Ability to work independently under high-pressure situations

**Preferred Qualifications:**
- 🌟 Certification in Crisis Intervention (CPI, ASIST)
- 🌟 Previous crisis line experience (988, Crisis Text Line)
- 🌟 Background in CBT, DBT, or trauma-informed care
- 🌟 Bilingual (Spanish/Mandarin/etc.)

**Responsibilities:**
- Review automated crisis alerts (within 5 minutes for Level 1)
- Conduct telephonic risk assessments
- Determine appropriate intervention level
- Make emergency referrals when needed
- Document all interventions in clinical notes
- Participate in on-call rotation

**Hours:**
- Minimum: 3 clinicians for 24/7 coverage (8-hour shifts)
- Recommended: 6+ clinicians for backup/sick coverage
- On-call rotation: 1 week at a time

---

### **2. Crisis Team Coordinator**

**Required Qualifications:**
- ✅ Clinical license OR 5+ years crisis management experience
- ✅ Program management experience
- ✅ Strong communication skills
- ✅ Ability to coordinate multiple moving parts

**Responsibilities:**
- Manage on-call schedule
- Ensure all alerts are acknowledged
- Coordinate with emergency services when needed
- Maintain crisis intervention protocols
- Train new team members
- Quality assurance and chart reviews

**Hours:**
- Full-time (40 hours/week)
- On-call for escalations

---

### **3. On-Call Psychiatrist (Medical Director)**

**Required Qualifications:**
- ✅ Medical Doctor (MD or DO) with psychiatry residency
- ✅ Board certification in Psychiatry
- ✅ Active medical license in all states where service is offered
- ✅ Hospital admitting privileges
- ✅ DEA registration for medications if applicable

**Responsibilities:**
- Medical consultation for complex cases
- Medication evaluation if treatment referral needed
- Hospital admission coordination
- Clinical supervision and consultation
- Protocol development and review

**Hours:**
- Part-time consulting basis
- On-call for high-risk cases

---

### **4. Crisis Support Specialists (Non-Clinical)**

**Required Qualifications:**
- ✅ Bachelor's degree in psychology, social work, or related field
- ✅ Crisis intervention training (ASIST, Mental Health First Aid)
- ✅ Excellent communication skills
- ✅ Emotional resilience

**Responsibilities:**
- Initial response to lower-risk alerts
- Resource provision and referrals
- Follow-up check-ins with users
- Data entry and documentation
- Escalation to licensed clinicians

**Hours:**
- Full-time positions
- Overlap with clinician shifts

### **Step 2: Screening & Interview**

**Resume Review Criteria:**
- ✅ Valid, unrestricted license
- ✅ Crisis experience keywords: "crisis," "suicide," "emergency," "988," "hotline"
- ✅ No malpractice claims or disciplinary actions
- ✅ Recent clinical practice (within last 2 years)

**Phone Screen (15 minutes):**
1. Confirm license status and availability
2. Explain on-call expectations
3. Verify crisis intervention experience
4. Discuss comfort with remote crisis work

**Technical Interview (45 minutes):**

**Question 1:** "Walk me through how you would assess someone who screened positive for recent suicide attempt."

**Good Answer:**
- Immediate safety assessment (current plan, means, intent)
- Determine if emergency services needed
- Assess protective factors
- Collaborate on safety plan
- Ensure not left alone

**Question 2:** "You receive an automated alert at 3am for a user with severe depression and suicide ideation. What do you do?"

**Good Answer:**
- Review screening results immediately
- Attempt contact within 5 minutes
- If no answer, try emergency contact
- Document all attempts
- Escalate according to protocol
- Never ignore an alert

**Question 3:** "How do you handle your own emotional regulation after responding to a crisis?"

**Good Answer:**
- Self-awareness of secondary trauma
- Debrief with supervisor
- Self-care practices
- Know when to take break

**Role-Play Scenario:**
"You receive a Level 1 alert. The user is a 28-year-old male, PHQ-9 score 26, C-SSRS indicates recent suicide attempt. You call the phone number. What do you say?"

**Evaluate:**
- Immediate rapport building
- Direct questions about safety
- Clear, calm communication
- Appropriate next steps

---

### **Step 3: Background Checks**

**Required Checks:**
1. **License Verification**
   - Verify with state licensing board
   - Check for disciplinary actions
   - Confirm license is active and unrestricted

2. **Malpractice History**
   - National Practitioner Data Bank (NPDB) query
   - Search court records for malpractice suits
   - Verify current malpractice insurance

3. **Criminal Background Check**
   - FBI fingerprint check
   - State criminal history
   - Sex offender registry

4. **Professional References**
   - Contact 3 clinical supervisors
   - Ask specifically about crisis work
   - Verify employment dates and responsibilities

5. **Education Verification**
   - Confirm degrees from accredited institutions
   - Verify license exam completion

---

### **Step 4: Orientation & Training**

**Week 1: Orientation**
- Day 1: Platform training (how to use PsychSync system)
- Day 2: Crisis protocols and escalation procedures
- Day 3: Documentation requirements (HIPAA, clinical notes)
- Day 4: Role-play scenarios with senior clinician
- Day 5: Shadow senior clinicians on live alerts

**Week 2: Supervised Practice**
- Respond to real alerts with supervision
- Daily chart reviews
- Feedback on assessments
- Build confidence

**Week 3: Independent Practice**
- Solo on lower-risk alerts
- Supervisor available for consultation
- Weekly group supervision

**Week 4: Full Integration**
- Full on-call responsibilities
- Weekly supervision continues
- Monthly quality improvement reviews

---

**Minimum Schedule (3 clinicians):**
```
Week 1: Clinician A (Mon 8am - Mon 8am)
Week 2: Clinician B (Mon 8am - Mon 8am)
Week 3: Clinician C (Mon 8am - Mon 8am)
Repeat
```

**Recommended Schedule (6 clinicians):**
```
Week 1: Clinician A (Primary), Clinician D (Backup)
Week 2: Clinician B (Primary), Clinician E (Backup)
Week 3: Clinician C (Primary), Clinician F (Backup)
Repeat
```

**On-Call Expectations:**
- Must respond to alerts within 5 minutes
- Must be sober, awake, and available
- Can work remotely from home
- Paid hourly for being on-call + per-response stipend
- 1-week on, 2-3 weeks off

## 📊 Key Performance Metrics

### **Response Time**
- **Target:** < 5 minutes for Level 1 (critical)
- **Target:** < 2 hours for Level 2 (high)
- **Target:** < 24 hours for Level 3 (moderate)

### **Alert Acknowledgment Rate**
- **Target:** 100% of critical alerts acknowledged
- **Target:** 95% of high alerts acknowledged

### **Follow-Through Rate**
- **Target:** 100% of critical cases result in intervention
- **Target:** 90% of high-risk cases connect with care

### **Clinician Satisfaction**
- **Target:** < 20% turnover annually
- **Target:** > 4/5 satisfaction score

## 🚨 Red Flags to Avoid

### **During Hiring:**
❌ Unwilling to work nights/weekends
❌ No crisis intervention experience
❌ Poor boundary maintenance
❌ History of malpractice or disciplinary action
❌ Cannot handle emotional intensity
❌ Rigid or judgmental attitude

### **During Employment:**
❌ Consistently missing 5-minute response window
❌ Poor documentation habits
❌ Burnout signs (cynicism, exhaustion)
❌ Ignoring protocols
❌ Ethical boundary violations
