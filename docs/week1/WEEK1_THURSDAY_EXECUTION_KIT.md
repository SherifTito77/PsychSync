**Option A: Approve as-is** ⭐ (Recommended)

  - Aligns team around common goal
  - Competitive (establishes PsychSync as market leader)
- **Cons:**

- **Vote:** [ ] Approve

- **Description:** Adjust vision based on feedback (e.g., 2027 target = $40M ARR, not $50M)
- **Pros:**
  - More conservative (reduces risk)
  - Allows for execution slip-ups
  - May be more realistic given market uncertainty
- **Cons:**
  - Less ambitious (less attractive to investors)
  - Demotivating (team feels lack of confidence)
  - May leave money on the table (if we can hit $50M)

- **Cons:**

### Decision 5: Approve Risk Mitigation Plan + Assign Owners

**Context:**
See RISK_MITIGATION_OWNER_ASSIGNMENTS.md for full risk details.

**The Question:**
"Do we approve the risk mitigation plan and assign owners?"

**The 4 Critical Risks:**
- **Risk 1: Scientific Validity** → Owner: Head of Data Science
  - **Risk:** "Is this real science or personality astrology?"
  - **Impact:** High (if invalid, product has no value)
  - **Probability:** Medium (Big Five is valid, but our implementation may not be)
  - **Mitigation:** Hire PhD psychometrician, publish whitepapers, validate algorithm

- **Risk 2: Data Breach** → Owner: CTO/CISO
  - **Risk:** "Will you leak my team's personality data?"
  - **Impact:** High (destroys trust, legal liability)
  - **Probability:** Medium (we have good security but not enterprise-grade yet)
  - **Mitigation:** Encryption at rest, penetration testing, audit logging, SOC 2 certification

- **Risk 3: ML Model Accuracy** → Owner: Head of ML
  - **Risk:** "Are your conflict predictions accurate?"
  - **Impact:** High (if inaccurate, customers won't trust us)
  - **Probability:** Medium (we have good models but they need validation)
  - **Mitigation:** Confidence intervals, retraining pipeline, model monitoring, A/B testing

- **Risk 4: Regulatory Compliance** → Owner: General Counsel
  - **Risk:** "Is this legal for hiring decisions?"
  - **Impact:** High (if non-compliant, lawsuits, fines, reputational damage)
  - **Probability:** Medium (we're compliant today but employment law is complex)
  - **Mitigation:** Geofencing (block hiring features in NYC, California, EU), bias audits, legal review

**Decision Options:**

**Option A: Approve risk owners and mitigation plans** ⭐ (Recommended)
- **Description:** Approve risk assignments (Head of DS, CTO, Head of ML, GC) and mitigation plans
- **Pros:**
  - Clear ownership (each risk has a responsible owner)
  - Actionable (each risk has Week 1 actions)
  - Measurable (we can track risk scores weekly)
  - Reassuring (shows we're proactively managing risks)
- **Cons:**
  - Time-consuming (owners spend 20% time on risk mitigation)
  - Expensive (hiring PhD psychometrician, penetration testing, etc.)
  - May be overkill (some risks may be overblown)
- **Risk:** Low
- **Vote:** [ ] Approve risk owners and mitigation plans

**Option B: Approve with owner changes**
- **Description:** Change risk owners (e.g., CTO should own Scientific Validity, not Head of DS)
- **Pros:**
  - Better alignment (maybe CEO should own all risks?)
  - More accountability (exec owners vs. individual contributors)
- **Cons:**
  - Less expertise (CTO may not understand psychometric validity)
  - Less capacity (execs already busy)
- **Risk:** Low
- **Vote:** [ ] Approve with owner changes (specify: __________________)

**Option C: Reject, assign different owners**
- **Description:** Risk mitigation plan is wrong (wrong owners, wrong mitigations)
- **Pros:**
  - Reassesses ownership (maybe we need a Chief Risk Officer?)
  - May identify better structure
- **Cons:**
  - Delays execution (risk owners can't start Week 1 actions)
  - Demotivating (team spent time on mitigation plans)
- **Risk:** Medium
- **Vote:** [ ] Reject, assign different owners (specify: __________________)

**Decision Rubric:**
- **Owner Expertise:** Does owner have expertise to mitigate this risk? (Head of DS knows psychometrics, CTO knows security)
- **Owner Capacity:** Does owner have time to mitigate this risk? (They're already busy—can they add 20% more?)
- **Owner Accountability:** Is owner incentivized to mitigate risk? (Is this in their OKRs?)
- **Mitigation Quality:** Are mitigation plans realistic? (Can we hire PhD psychometrician in Q1?)
- **Risk Interdependencies:** Do these risks compound? (Scientific invalidity + regulatory compliance = dangerous)
- **Risk Transparency:** Are we being transparent about risks with team, investors, customers?

**CEO Recommendation:**
"I recommend Option A (Approve risk owners and mitigation plans). Each owner has expertise (Head of DS knows psychometrics, CTO knows security). Each owner has capacity (they'll spend 20% time on risk mitigation). Each mitigation plan is realistic (hire PhD psychometrician in Q1, penetration testing in Q1). Risk reviews (Fridays 3 PM) will keep us accountable. Alternative (Option B, exec owners) is worse—execs don't have expertise or capacity. We need individual contributors to own these risks."

**Vote:** [ ] A (Approve risk owners and mitigation plans) / [ ] B (Approve with owner changes) / [ ] C (Reject)

## 🎯 THURSDAY LEARNINGS

**Executive Review Insights:**
- **Why 5 Decisions in 2 Hours?** Decision fatigue is real. If we spread decisions over multiple meetings, we'll never decide. Concentrating decisions in 2 hours forces trade-off thinking and prevents procrastination.
- **Why 20-Min Presentations?** Time constraints force clarity. You can't ramble if you only have 20 minutes. You must distill to the most critical points: vision, roadmap, GTM, targets.
- **Why Vote?** Voting builds commitment. If everyone votes "yes," they're bought in. If they vote "no," they've identified risks. Consensus (or lack thereof) reveals alignment gaps.

**Decision-Making Framework:**
- **Option A (Approve)** is the recommendation. This biases action over analysis. We're builders, not philosophers. We should default to "yes" unless there's a compelling reason to say "no."
- **Option B (Modify)** is the compromise. It preserves the spirit of the proposal while adjusting the details. Use this if you agree with the direction but disagree with the specifics.
- **Option C (Reject)** is the nuclear option. Use this only if the proposal is fundamentally flawed (wrong direction, wrong assumptions, wrong numbers).

**Risk Mitigation:**
- **Why Assign Owners?** "Everyone's responsibility is no one's responsibility." By assigning 4 critical risks to 4 owners, we ensure accountability. Each owner spends 20% time on risk mitigation.
- **Why Weekly Reviews?** Risks evolve. What's a medium risk today may be a high risk next week. Weekly reviews (Fridays 3 PM) keep risks visible and manageable.
