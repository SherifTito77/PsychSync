# Malpractice Insurance Guide

**Purpose:** Understand malpractice insurance requirements for PsychSync crisis clinicians

**⚠️ CRITICAL:** Malpractice insurance protects clinicians and the organization from liability claims

---

## 🎯 What is Malpractice Insurance?

**Professional Liability Insurance** (malpractice insurance) protects healthcare providers against claims of negligence or harm resulting from professional services.

**For PsychSync:**
- Covers crisis clinicians providing intervention
- Protects organization from vicarious liability
- Required by most state licensing boards
- Required byPsychSync for all clinical staff

---

## 📋 Coverage Requirements

### **Minimum Coverage Required**

**Per Occurrence Limit:** $1,000,000
- Covers claims arising from a single incident

**Aggregate Limit:** $3,000,000
- Total coverage for all claims in a policy year

**Rationale:**
- Industry standard for mental health professionals
- Sufficient to cover most claims and legal fees
- Required by many healthcare facilities
- Recommended by risk management experts

---

### **What Must Be Covered**

**Covered Services:**
✅ Crisis intervention consultations
✅ Suicide risk assessments
✅ Safety planning
✅ Referrals to emergency services
✅ Documentation and clinical records
✅ Telephone/video interventions
✅ Consultation with other clinicians

**Covered Locations:**
✅ Remote/work-from-home
✅ All states where clinicians are licensed
✅ All states where users are located (check policy)
✅ International (if applicable - check policy)

## 🏢 Insurance Options

### **Option 1: Individual Clinician Policies**

**Clinicians purchase their own malpractice insurance**

**Pros:**
✅ Coverage follows clinician (not tied to employer)
✅ Clinician has control over policy
✅ Can be tailored to clinician's specific practice
✅ Portable if clinician leaves PsychSync

**Cons:**
❌ Higher cost for individual policies
❌ Admin overhead to verify everyone has coverage
❌ May not cover organization vicariously
❌ Clinicians responsible for renewals

**Cost:**
- Part-time contractors: $1,000-2,000/year
- Full-time clinicians: $2,000-5,000/year

**Recommended Providers:**
- CPH & Associates (cphins.com)
- HPSO (hppo.com)
- Aon (aon.com)
- NASW Assurance Services (for social workers)

---

### **Option 2: Organization Policy (Corporate Coverage)**

**PsychSync purchases one policy covering all clinicians**

**Pros:**
✅ Lower cost per clinician (bulk pricing)
✅ Guaranteed coverage for all staff
✅ Includes vicarious liability for organization
✅ Easier administration (one policy)
✅ Consistent coverage across all clinicians

**Cons:**
❌ Coverage doesn't follow clinician if they leave
❌ Organization has to pay all premiums
❌ May be harder to get approved (depends on org risk profile)
❌ Clinicians have no individual policy

**Cost:**
- Approx. $5,000-15,000/year for 3-5 clinicians
- Scales with number of clinicians and claims history

**Recommended Providers:**
- HPSO (Group Practice)
- CPH & Associates (Group)
- Aon (Professional Liability)
- Berkley Alternative Risk (for startups)

---

### **Option 3: Hybrid Approach (RECOMMENDED)**

**PsychSync provides corporate policy + Clinicians maintain individual policies**

**Pros:**
✅ Maximum protection (dual coverage)
✅ Organization protected even if clinician's policy lapses
✅ Clinicians have their own coverage for side work
✅ Defense in depth approach

**Cons:**
❌ Higher total cost
❌ Some redundancy in coverage

**Cost:**
- Organization policy: $5,000-10,000/year
- Individual policies: Clinicians pay their own (stipend optional)

## 📝 Policy Requirements

### **Essential Policy Provisions**

**1. Claims-Made vs. Occurrence**

**Claims-Made Policy:**
- Covers claims made while policy is active
- Must purchase "tail coverage" when leaving
- Lower initial premiums
- More common for organizations

**Occurrence Policy:**
- Covers claims that occurred during policy period, regardless of when claim is made
- No tail coverage needed
- Higher initial premiums
- Better for long-term protection

**Recommendation:** Occurrence policy if budget allows

---

**2. Consent to Settle**

**Must Include:** Insurer cannot settle claim without clinician's consent

**Why:** Protects clinician's reputation and license
- Some policies allow insurer to settle regardless
- This could result in admission of liability
- Could damage clinician's career

**Requirement:** Ensure policy has "consent to settle" clause

---

**3. Licensing Board Defense**

**Must Include:** Coverage for licensing board complaints

**Why:** Malpractice claims often trigger board complaints
- Board investigations are expensive
- Need attorney specializing in administrative law
- Standard malpractice may not cover this

**Requirement:** Add endorsement for licensing board defense

---

**4. Telehealth/Remote Services**

**Must Include:** Coverage for telephone/video interventions

**Why:** Crisis intervention is 100% remote
- Traditional policies may only cover in-person
- Remote services may be excluded or limited

**Requirement:** Verify telehealth is included, not excluded

---

**5. Crisis Intervention Specific**

**Must Include:** Coverage for crisis intervention and suicide prevention

**Why:** Some policies exclude high-risk interventions
- Suicide intervention may be considered "high-risk"
- Emergency services may have special rules

**Requirement:** Explicitly include crisis intervention

---

**6. Cyber Liability**

**Should Include:** Coverage for data breaches involving PHI

**Why:** HIPAA breaches can result in malpractice claims
- If PHI is exposed, users may sue
- Cyber policy covers breach response + liability

**Recommendation:** Add cyber liability endorsement

## 🔍 Selecting an Insurance Provider

### **Questions to Ask**

**1. Do you cover mental health crisis intervention services?**
- Must be yes

**2. Do you cover remote/telehealth services?**
- Must be yes

**3. Is this a claims-made or occurrence policy?**
- Know what you're buying

**4. Can you add "consent to settle" clause?**
- Must be yes

**5. What's the process for adding new clinicians?**
- Should be simple/fast

**6. Do you offer discounts for risk management practices?**
- Ask about credits for training, protocols, etc.

**7. What's the claims history of similar organizations?**
- Understand your risk category

**8. Do you provide risk management resources?**
- Free CMEs, guidelines, etc.

**9. What's the financial strength rating?**
- A.M. Best rating of A- or higher

**10. How long have you been insuring mental health professionals?**
- Experience matters

---

### **Red Flags**

❌ Refuses to cover crisis intervention
❌ No experience with mental health liability
❌ Poor financial rating (B+ or below)
❌ Long history of denied claims
❌ Exclusions for telehealth
❌ No consent to settle clause
❌ Extremely low premiums (too good to be true)
❌ Poor customer service/reviews

## ⚖️ Vicarious Liability

### **What is Vicarious Liability?**

**Legal doctrine:** Employer is responsible for employee's actions within scope of employment

**For PsychSync:**
- If clinician makes error during crisis intervention
- AND user sues both clinician AND PsychSync
- PsychSync is vicariously liable for clinician's actions

---

### **How Insurance Protects Against Vicarious Liability**

**Organization Policy:**
✅ Explicitly covers vicarious liability
✅ Pays for PsychSync's defense if sued for clinician's actions
✅ Pays damages if clinician is found negligent

**Individual Policy:**
✅ Covers clinician personally
✅ May NOT cover PsychSync vicariously (depends on policy)
✅ PsychSync may need separate coverage

**Recommendation:** Have organization policy regardless of individual policies

## 🚨 Claims Process

### **If a Claim Arises:**

**Step 1: Immediate Notice**
- Notify insurance carrier immediately (within 24-48 hours)
- Do NOT admit fault or liability
- Do NOT discuss the case with anyone except insurer and attorney

**Step 2: Preserve Evidence**
- Secure all records (screenings, notes, call recordings)
- Do NOT alter or destroy anything
- Document timeline of events

**Step 3: Legal Counsel**
- Insurance will assign attorney
- Work with attorney on defense strategy
- Attend all meetings and depositions

**Step 4: Resolution**
- Most claims settle out of court
- Some go to trial (rare)
- Insurance pays defense costs + damages (up to policy limits)

---

### **Common Claim Scenarios**

**Scenario 1: Suicide After Screening**
- User screened negative, later died by suicide
- Family claims PsychSync should have detected risk
- Defense: Screening tools have limitations; not diagnostic

**Scenario 2: Failed Crisis Intervention**
- User screened positive, clinician contacted but didn't escalate
- User harmed self
- Defense: Clinician followed protocol; assessment was reasonable

**Scenario 3: Breach of Confidentiality**
- PHI disclosed improperly
- User sues for privacy violation
- Defense: Disclosure was permitted under emergency exception

**Scenario 4: Negligent Referral**
- Clinician referred to inappropriate provider
- User harmed by referred provider
- Defense: Referral was reasonable; not liable for referred provider's actions
